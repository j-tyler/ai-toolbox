# Change Intent

The goal is to construct changes in a way that they're **reviewable**: less focus on the change itself, more focus on the review process around it. By the time a change reaches human review, it carries a visible chain of custody: an approved intent, the resulting implementation and tests, any implementation-time amendments, completion against the goal, and an independent review assessment. This gives the reviewer a consistent frame for how AI developed and assessed the change and where human judgment is most valuable. Today that means reviewing better; over time, it means reviewing less. This is an idea related to [Working in Public](../working-in-public/README.md) — the principle of capturing the high-value outputs of AI work in artifacts that persist beyond the session that produced them, so future agents can reference them; change intent does this for the pre-code dialogue that figures out what a change should be.

A change intent is a structured artifact whose initial form is approved *before implementation begins*. It is **complete over change-defining decisions and open over implementation**: it records the outcomes, guarantees, constraints, and exclusions that distinguish the change without attempting to prescribe every technical choice or incidental effect. A fork is change-defining only when choosing between its branches decides which change will be delivered; if the author could approve once and allow either branch while still receiving the approved change, the fork is implementation latitude. Completeness is the standard for the artifact and the authoring process, not a claim that an agent can prove the absence of unknown dependencies. The implementation agent may record a narrow amendment if a claim cannot be delivered within the approved boundaries or implementation reveals a necessary change-defining decision that the approved intent does not settle. If returned work changes the author's direction before merge, the author may replace the current unmerged intent; the replacement is approved and implementation and review run again against it. Only the intent that merges becomes the persistent record.

## Adoption fit

Change intent is intended for teams that delegate material implementation or review work to AI agents and want the decisions behind a change to remain independent of the code that happens to be produced. Adoption requires the team to approve an initial intent before implementation begins, reassess retained work whenever the author replaces an unmerged intent, and preserve merged intents as historical records. It is not a process for exploratory work, a substitute for ordinary engineering review, or a template to be backfilled after implementation.

Adopting the process assigns each role explicitly. The author decides the change-defining questions surfaced before approval. The implementation agent may choose unconstrained technical details without further approval and may use the amendment process when implementation proves that an approved claim cannot be delivered within the approved boundaries or exposes a missing necessary decision. A missing-decision amendment is a provisional implementation-time ruling that the author reviews when the work returns. The reviewer independently assesses the implementation and its evidence, using the capabilities available in the team's review operation and inference to determine whether each acceptance criterion has a proving test and whether each invariant holds across the affected change. `Cannot verify` is a valid review result that identifies the limit of the available evidence, not a failure of the review process. These role boundaries allow work to continue without turning the intent into an exhaustive specification.

**Outcomes** and **Why** are always included: what the change is intended to make true, and the problem, event, or need that caused the change to be made. Why explains the change; it does not establish requirements or implementation direction.

The change intent file also includes any of the following when their conditions apply:

- **Constraints** — conditions and non-behavioral boundaries that every acceptable implementation must be designed around. They guide implementation and review but are not automatically proof obligations; lack of proof is not a violation.
- **Acceptance criteria** — falsifiable scenarios that prove selected outcomes, guarantees, or guardrails, each closed by a focused proving test. "When a user does X, the system returns Y." They are proof obligations for the decisions the change makes, not an inventory of everything implementation may make observable.
- **Invariants** — properties that must hold *across* the parts of the system affected by the change. Tests should exercise them where useful, but no passing test closes an invariant by itself; implementation and review must also reason across the affected diff and relevant surrounding paths. The intent states the property, not an inventory of locations or tests. "Read-after-write holds across all caller paths of `GetUser`."
- **Out of scope** — what this change explicitly is *not* doing. Signals conscious exclusions to the implementation agent, the AI review pass, and the human reviewer. Applies when there are conscious exclusions worth signaling.
- **Amendments** — repairs made during implementation, when a claim could not be delivered within the approved boundaries or a necessary change-defining decision surfaced after approval. Each entry records the discovered fact and quotes the affected item's previous and current wording. The current intent remains understandable without reading this history. The author rules on any amendment when the work returns.

Each section serves a distinct downstream purpose: Why carries motivation to future readers; acceptance criteria are mechanically checked test-by-test; invariants invite property thinking across the diff; constraints bound engineering judgment without demanding proof that the available environment cannot provide; out of scope signals conscious exclusion to every downstream consumer; amendments record how the approved intent changed when implementation exposed an error or missing decision. An omitted section indicates that its condition does not apply.
